What is SWOT Analysis?

SWOT analysis is a strategic planning tool used to evaluate Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats related to a decision or problem.

Strengths

Internal advantages that help you succeed.

Weaknesses

Internal challenges that could hold you back.

Opportunities

External trends or possibilities you can take advantage of.

Threats

External risks or obstacles you should prepare for.

Why SWOT Analysis is Useful

SWOT helps teams and leaders get clear about what matters most and make stronger decisions.

The Four Components of SWOT

Strengths

Internal advantages the organization has.

  • Strong brand
  • Skilled employees
  • Efficient operations

Weaknesses

Areas where the organization can improve.

  • Limited resources
  • Outdated systems
  • Poor visibility

Opportunities

External trends that can be leveraged.

  • Market growth
  • New partnerships
  • Emerging technology

Threats

External risks that can impact success.

  • New competitors
  • Regulatory change
  • Economic shifts

Internal vs External Factors

SWOT is a mix of internal and external factors. Knowing the difference helps you focus the right questions.

Internal Factors

  • Strengths
  • Weaknesses

External Factors

  • Opportunities
  • Threats

Example SWOT Analysis

Scenario: A company considering adopting AI tools.

Strengths

  • Strong technical team
  • Access to company data

Weaknesses

  • Limited AI expertise
  • Training costs

Opportunities

  • Automation improvements
  • Productivity gains

Threats

  • Fast-moving technology
  • Employee resistance

How to Do a SWOT Analysis

Follow these steps to create your own SWOT analysis.

  1. Define the problem or decision. Clarify the goal or challenge you want to explore.
  2. Identify internal strengths. List what you do well and what gives you an advantage.
  3. Identify internal weaknesses. Be honest about what holds you back.
  4. Look for external opportunities. Find trends or situations you can use.
  5. Identify external threats. Note risks that could affect your plan.
  6. Use insights to plan actions. Turn your findings into clear next steps.
